## SECTION 1 — Scope and Purpose

### 1.1 Purpose

This illustrative protocol establishes fictional monitoring workflows for tracking player training load, wellness, sleep, soreness, and fatigue within a hypothetical professional football club. Its purpose within this software prototype is to demonstrate how a document-grounded AI system might retrieve relevant policy sections when evaluating a player's risk profile.

### 1.2 Scope

This fictional protocol covers:
- Acute and chronic training load monitoring
- Sleep quality tracking
- Wellness and fatigue screening
- Soreness assessment
- Workload change thresholds
- Injury-risk escalation procedures (illustrative)
- Medical review triggers

### 1.3 Governing Principle

**All player health decisions are made exclusively by qualified medical professionals.** No automated system, AI tool, or risk score shall replace clinical judgment. Risk flags are advisory only.

## SECTION 2 — Training Load Monitoring

### 2.1 Acute Training Load Definition

Acute training load refers to the rolling 7-day sum or weighted average of session RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion) multiplied by session duration in minutes. It represents the short-term physiological demand placed on the player.

**Illustrative Thresholds (Fictional):**
| Load Level | Acute Load Score | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Low | < 1,000 AU | Normal monitoring |
| Moderate | 1,000–2,000 AU | Standard check-in |
| High | 2,001–3,000 AU | Mandatory wellness review |
| Very High | > 3,000 AU | Immediate medical flag |

### 2.2 Chronic Training Load Definition

Chronic training load represents the rolling 28-day average of training load, reflecting the player's long-term fitness base and adaptation level.

**Illustrative Thresholds (Fictional):**
| Load Level | Chronic Load Score | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Underprepared | < 800 AU | Gradual load increase plan |
| Optimal Base | 800–1,800 AU | Normal training |
| High Base | > 1,800 AU | Weekly medical review |

### 2.3 Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR)

The ACWR is calculated as Acute Load ÷ Chronic Load. This illustrative protocol uses ACWR as a supplementary signal, not a standalone predictor.

**Illustrative ACWR Thresholds (Fictional — NOT clinically validated for this system):**
| ACWR Range | Risk Signal | Recommended Action |
|---|---|---|
| < 0.8 | Underload signal | Discuss with strength & conditioning |
| 0.8–1.3 | Acceptable range | Continue normal monitoring |
| 1.3–1.5 | Elevated signal | Initiate daily wellness check |
| > 1.5 | High-load spike signal | Trigger medical review protocol |

> **Note:** Research on ACWR as an injury predictor is contested. This system treats it as one of multiple monitoring signals, not a causal predictor.

## SECTION 3 — Workload Change Thresholds

### 3.1 Week-on-Week Load Increase Limits

Sudden spikes in training load are associated with elevated fatigue and physiological stress. This fictional protocol uses conservative illustrative thresholds.

**Illustrative Weekly Load Increase Rules (Fictional):**

- **< 10% increase:** Normal progression — continue standard monitoring.
- **10–20% increase:** Flag for strength & conditioning review within 48 hours.
- **21–30% increase:** Mandatory wellness screening before next training session. Coach notification required.
- **> 30% increase:** Immediate escalation to medical team. Training load must be reviewed and adjusted. Player should not proceed with planned session without medical clearance.

### 3.2 Sudden Load Decrease

A sudden decrease of more than 40% from chronic load may indicate injury, illness, or deliberate tapering. This should be documented and reviewed.

### 3.3 Return-to-Training Protocol (Illustrative)

Players returning from any lay-off of more than 5 days must follow a graduated return:
- Days 1–2: Maximum 50% of typical session load
- Days 3–4: Maximum 70% of typical session load
- Day 5+: Full session subject to medical clearance

## SECTION 4 — Sleep Monitoring Protocol

### 4.1 Sleep Tracking

Sleep duration and quality are recorded daily via player self-report or wearable device. Both duration and subjective quality are tracked.

### 4.2 Illustrative Sleep Thresholds (Fictional)

| Sleep Duration | Status | Action |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 8 hours | Optimal | No action |
| 6–7.9 hours | Acceptable | Monitor over 3-day window |
| 4–5.9 hours | Poor | Wellness screening required; consider session modification |
| < 4 hours | Critical | Mandatory medical check-in before training; consider rest day |

### 4.3 Consecutive Poor Sleep

If a player reports fewer than 6 hours of sleep for **3 or more consecutive nights**, the sports medicine team must be notified regardless of other wellness indicators.

### 4.4 Sleep and Workload Interaction

Poor sleep (< 6 hours) combined with high acute training load (> 2,000 AU) is treated as a compound risk signal requiring same-day wellness assessment.

## SECTION 5 — Wellness and Fatigue Screening

### 5.1 Daily Wellness Questionnaire

Players complete a daily wellness questionnaire scored on a 0–10 scale for each of the following domains:

- **Soreness Score (0–10):** 0 = no soreness, 10 = severe soreness
- **Fatigue Score (0–10):** 0 = fully recovered, 10 = extreme fatigue
- **Wellness Score (0–10):** 0 = very poor wellness, 10 = excellent wellness

### 5.2 Soreness Thresholds (Illustrative)

| Soreness Score | Classification | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Normal | Continue training |
| 4–6 | Moderate | Reduce session intensity by 20%; physio review |
| 7–8 | High | Modified training only; physiotherapy appointment |
| 9–10 | Severe | Rest day; mandatory medical assessment |

### 5.3 Fatigue Thresholds (Illustrative)

| Fatigue Score | Classification | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Well recovered | Continue training |
| 4–5 | Mild fatigue | Monitor; consider session modification |
| 6–7 | Moderate fatigue | Reduce load; physio and nutritionist input |
| 8–10 | Severe fatigue | Immediate medical team notification; rest protocol |

### 5.4 Wellness Score Thresholds (Illustrative)

| Wellness Score | Classification | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 7–10 | Good wellness | Normal training |
| 4–6 | Borderline | Wellness review; discuss with player |
| 0–3 | Poor wellness | Mandatory medical review; do not increase load |

### 5.5 Composite Wellness Flag

If **two or more** of the following conditions are met simultaneously, a composite wellness flag is raised regardless of individual thresholds:
- Soreness ≥ 6
- Fatigue ≥ 6
- Wellness ≤ 4
- Sleep < 6 hours

A composite wellness flag triggers mandatory medical team notification within 2 hours.

## SECTION 6 — Previous Injury and Medical History Considerations

### 6.1 Previous Injury Flag

Players with a documented injury in the previous 8 weeks are classified as "recently injured" and are subject to enhanced monitoring protocols.

**Enhanced Monitoring Rules (Illustrative):**
- ACWR must remain below 1.2 (vs 1.5 for fully fit players)
- Weekly load increase must not exceed 15% (vs 30% for fully fit players)
- Physio sign-off required before any high-intensity session

### 6.2 Recurring Injury Pattern

If a player sustains an injury to the same anatomical site within 12 months of a previous injury, a full medical panel review is mandated before return to team training.

### 6.3 Injury History and Risk Scoring

Previous injury status is one factor in the overall wellness risk assessment. It does not automatically restrict training; clinical context determines restrictions.

## SECTION 7 — Escalation and Medical Review Procedures

### 7.1 Standard Monitoring (Green Status)

- No risk flags triggered
- Daily wellness questionnaire completed
- Results reviewed by performance data analyst
- No immediate action required

### 7.2 Advisory Flag (Amber Status)

Triggered when any of the following occur:
- ACWR between 1.3 and 1.5
- Single wellness dimension in "moderate" range
- Weekly load increase between 21–30%
- Sleep between 4–5.9 hours for one night

**Actions:**
- Performance data analyst notifies head physio within 4 hours
- Player wellness discussion conducted within 24 hours
- Training session may proceed with modifications
- Flag documented in player wellness log

### 7.3 Medical Review Trigger (Red Status)

Triggered when any of the following occur:
- ACWR > 1.5
- Soreness ≥ 9 or Fatigue ≥ 8
- Wellness ≤ 2
- Sleep < 4 hours
- Weekly load increase > 30%
- Composite wellness flag raised
- Player self-reports concern

**Actions:**
- Immediate notification to club doctor and head physio
- Training session paused pending medical assessment
- Player moved to individual monitoring
- Medical review documented within 2 hours
- Decision on training participation made by medical team only

### 7.4 Emergency Protocol

Any player reporting acute pain, chest discomfort, dizziness, or neurological symptoms during training must be removed from training immediately. Emergency medical services contacted per standard emergency procedure.

## SECTION 8 — Recovery Protocols

### 8.1 Standard Recovery Recommendations (Illustrative)

These are illustrative, not evidence-based prescriptions:
- **Hydration:** ≥ 2.5L water on training days (player self-report)
- **Nutrition:** Post-session protein intake within 30 minutes (dietitian guidance)
- **Sleep:** Target 8+ hours per night
- **Cold therapy/contrast baths:** Available on request; physio guidance required
- **Active recovery sessions:** Schedule as agreed with strength & conditioning team

### 8.2 Post-Match Recovery Window

Following a competitive match, a mandatory 48-hour reduced-load window applies. High-intensity training is not scheduled in this period without medical waiver.

### 8.3 Travel and Schedule Congestion

During periods of schedule congestion (3+ matches in 9 days), weekly load targets are reduced by 25% and daily wellness screening is mandatory for all squad members.

## SECTION 9 — Data Governance and Privacy (Illustrative)

### 9.1 Data Access

Player health data is accessible only to:
- Club doctor and physiotherapy team
- Head coach (summary reports only)
- Performance data analyst (anonymized aggregates)

### 9.2 Data Retention

Player health records are retained for the duration of the player's contract plus 7 years, per illustrative club policy.

### 9.3 AI System Disclosure

Players are informed in writing that an AI-assisted monitoring tool is used to generate risk flags. Players are informed that:
- The AI system does not make medical decisions
- All risk flags are reviewed by a qualified professional
- Players may request review of any automated flag

### 9.4 Consent and Opt-Out

Players sign informed consent before participating in wellness monitoring. Players may raise concerns about monitoring practices with the club's Player Welfare Officer.

## SECTION 10 — Limitations of This Protocol

### 10.1 Synthetic Nature

This protocol is **entirely fictional**. All thresholds, procedures, and rules are hypothetical and have not been validated in real clinical or sports environments.

### 10.2 Individual Variation

No fixed threshold applies universally to all athletes. Age, training history, position, playing style, and individual physiology all affect appropriate load targets. These must be determined individually by qualified practitioners.

### 10.3 AI Tool Limitations

The AI system supporting this protocol:
- Uses synthetic training data only
- Has not been validated on real player populations
- May produce false positives and false negatives
- Must not be used for actual medical decisions

### 10.4 Human Oversight Requirement

All outputs from any AI-assisted monitoring tool must be reviewed by a qualified medical professional before any clinical or training decision is made.
